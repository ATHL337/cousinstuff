recipeInstructions:
  - "Make stock: Combine the neck, heart, gizzard, 2 cups broth, water, onion, thyme, pepper and optional rosemary in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer for 60–90 minutes. Keep the solids mostly submerged, adding a little water if necessary. Leave the liver out for a milder stock."
  - "Strain: Pour through a fine strainer into a heat-safe container. Discard the spent solids or reserve fully cooked edible meat separately. Measure the stock and add turkey broth to reach 3 cups of liquid. If making in advance, cool and refrigerate promptly."
  - "Make the roux: Melt 4 1/2 tbsp butter over medium heat. Whisk in 4 1/2 tbsp flour and cook for 2–3 minutes, stirring constantly until lightly golden."
  - "Build the gravy: Gradually whisk in the 3 cups of liquid, starting with small additions to avoid lumps."
  - "Finish: Bring to a full boil while whisking, then reduce to a simmer for 3–5 minutes, until the gravy coats the back of a spoon. Taste before adding salt. Thin with broth a tablespoon at a time if needed."
  - "Serve warm over turkey, stuffing or potatoes."

## From the Vault kitchen

A holiday gravy should not depend on perfect pan drippings. During our [Wasteland Thanksgiving](/wasteland-thanksgiving/) cook, the neck-and-giblet stock yielded just under two cups and tasted peppery without being very salty. Our finished gravy was a family favorite and helped the slightly dry turkey breast.

**Yield:** About 3 cups, or 12 quarter-cup servings (estimated). The stock and gravy quantities above reconstruct the cooking plan; exact ingredient amounts used in the family cook were not independently measured in our notes. The optional rosemary reflects the herbs visible in the stock.

**Equipment:** Two medium saucepans (or wash one between steps), strainer, heat-safe measuring cup, whisk and ladle.

**Pan-dripping option:** Substitute clean, defatted, unburned turkey juices for some of the additional broth. Avoid scorched residue. Bring the finished gravy to a full boil; do not taste potentially undercooked drippings before boiling.

**Troubleshooting:** If too thick, whisk in warm broth. If too thin, simmer briefly. If too salty or peppery, dilute with unsalted broth. Do not add flour directly to finished gravy.

**Storage:** Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers within two hours, use within 3–4 days or freeze. Bring leftover gravy to a rolling boil when reheating. Whisk in broth to loosen as needed.
