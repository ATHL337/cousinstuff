## Overview

The Vault alarm says breakfast. The family schedule says dinner. **Vault 76 Sunrise Ration Wraps** settle the argument.

Instead of scrambling eggs and assembling a dozen separate fillings, this recipe turns crispy hash brown patties, breakfast sausage, eggs, and cheddar into a sliceable oven bake. Cut it into portions, roll each piece in a tortilla, and serve immediately or stock the freezer for another day.

**Theme:** Vault-Tec Field Kitchen  
**Profile:** Mild family base, filling breakfast-for-dinner, practical make-ahead option  
**Vibe:** Golden hash browns, savory sausage, fluffy eggs, melted cheese, warm toasted tortilla  

**Yield:** 8 hearty dinner burritos or 10 smaller meal-prep wraps. Recipe metadata uses 10 servings.

**Equipment:** 9×13-inch metal or ceramic baking pan with sides at least 2 inches high; skillet; mixing bowl; whisk; spatula; knife; food thermometer.

> **Field-kitchen rule:** Use a deep baking pan, not a flat cookie sheet. The eggs need walls to stay in the pan.

## Vault Notes

### Why the Hash Browns Go First

Frozen hash brown patties need a head start before the egg mixture goes in. Pre-crisping produces browned potato edges and keeps the bottom from becoming a wet layer of potatoes. Once eggs are added, the surface touching the custard softens somewhat; for extra crunch, toast the filled tortilla before serving.

### Why the Sausage Is Fully Cooked First

The egg layer cooks more evenly when the sausage is browned and drained before assembly. Raw sausage placed directly into the eggs can add excess grease and make it harder to judge whether every piece is done.

### Family Heat Control

Keep the main pan mild. Let each person choose what goes into the finished wrap:

- **Kid-friendly:** sausage, egg, hash brown, and cheese with mild salsa on the side.
- **Adult upgrade:** hot sauce, pickled jalapeños, green onion, or extra smoky seasoning.
- **Creamy dipping sauce:** stir together 1/2 cup Greek yogurt, 1/4 cup mild salsa, and 1–2 tsp lime juice. Serve on the side, not inside wraps destined for the freezer.

### S.P.E.C.I.A.L. Loadout Switches

- **S (Strength):** Add another 2 eggs for larger portions; allow additional oven time and verify the center reaches 165°F.
- **P (Perception):** Dice a bell pepper finely and cook it briefly with the sausage for color and sweetness.
- **E (Endurance):** Use a leaner fully cooked breakfast sausage and drain thoroughly.
- **C (Charisma):** Serve a build-your-own topping station so every family member gets their preferred level of heat.
- **I (Intelligence):** Mix the dipping sauce while the filling bakes; keep it separate for meal prep.
- **A (Agility):** For a quicker assembly, use fully cooked refrigerated sausage crumbles, heated according to package directions.
- **L (Luck):** Swap the sausage for about 8–10 strips of cooked, crumbled bacon for a different batch.

### Batch Size and Pan Choice

Use **8–10 patties**, not a fixed count regardless of brand. Arrange enough to cover most of the bottom without overlap. A 9×13-inch pan with at least 2-inch sides contains the egg mixture. For a larger batch, use two pans rather than overcrowding one shallow sheet pan.

## Breakfast-for-Dinner Deployment

For dinner tonight, cut the bake into **8 larger pieces**, roll in burrito-size tortillas, and toast each wrap seam-side down. Offer sliced fruit, avocado, or a simple green salad alongside. Keep salsa and hot sauce on the table so the main pan is suitable for everyone.

If making a freezer batch, cut into **10 smaller pieces** and skip wet toppings until serving.

## Storage + Reheating

- **Cool safely:** Rest the filling about 10 minutes and let excess steam escape before wrapping. Refrigerate or freeze promptly; do not leave cooked egg dishes at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
- **Refrigerator:** Wrap individually or store airtight at 40°F (4°C) or colder for **3–4 days**.
- **Freezer:** Wrap individually in parchment paper, put in a labeled freezer bag, and freeze. **Best quality within 2 months**; longer frozen storage may affect tortilla texture.
- **From the fridge:** Remove foil or other non-microwave-safe wrapping. Microwave about 1–2 minutes, turning halfway, until the center reaches **165°F (74°C)**.
- **From frozen:** Remove foil, cover loosely with a damp paper towel, and microwave in short intervals, generally 2–4 minutes depending on size and appliance. Turn halfway; check for **165°F** at the center.
- **For crisp edges:** After reheating completely, toast in a dry skillet for 1–2 minutes per side or briefly in an air fryer at 350°F. Do not rely on browning alone to verify a fully heated center.
- **Avoid sogginess:** Add salsa, yogurt sauce, avocado, or other wet toppings only after reheating.
