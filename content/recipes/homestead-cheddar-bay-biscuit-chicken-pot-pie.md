## Overview

This is a **family-size, CousinStuff-style adaptation** of [Moribyan's Cheddar Bay Biscuit Chicken Pot Pie](https://moribyan.com/cheddar-bay-biscuit-chicken-pot-pie/), published November 5, 2026. The inspiration is creamy chicken pot pie under browned cheddar-garlic drop biscuits, finished with garlic-herb butter. The CousinStuff version is scaled for a full family, uses a single casserole, lowers the default heat, and gives a streamlined kitchen flow.

**Theme:** Homestead comfort rations  
**Profile:** Creamy, savory, cheesy, mild, substantial  
**Family target:** 6 hearty or 8 moderate portions (four children plus adults; leftovers depend on appetites)  
**Status:** Adapted / **not yet family-tested**. Keep `draft: true` until a cook test confirms timing, portions, and preferred seasoning.  
**Timing:** About 30 minutes preparation, 20 minutes stovetop cooking, 30–35 minutes baking, and 10 minutes rest; allow **90–100 minutes** on the first cook.

## Equipment

### Required

- Standard stovetop and oven
- Large deep skillet, sauté pan, or Dutch oven (4–6 qt) to cook the filling
- Deep 9×13-inch baking dish, **ideally 3.5–4 qt**, or two smaller oven-safe baking dishes
- Rimmed sheet pan underneath the casserole to catch any overflow
- Large mixing bowl, small bowl, whisk or fork, stirring spoon or silicone spatula
- Knife, cutting board, measuring cups and spoons
- Large spoon or 1/4-cup measuring cup for dropping biscuit dough

### Helpful, not required

- Box grater for cold butter; alternatively cut butter into very small pieces with two forks
- Pastry brush for topping; alternatively drizzle with a spoon
- Instant-read thermometer to verify a reheated filling reaches 165°F (74°C)

**No individual cast-iron skillets, stand mixer, food processor, air fryer, or specialty biscuit cutters are needed.** For a shallow 9×13 dish, split into two dishes rather than letting filling touch the rim.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### 1. Set Up the Casserole and Chill the Biscuit Butter

Put **one stick of butter** for the biscuit topping into the freezer for **15–20 minutes**. You want it cold and firm, not permanently frozen solid.

Heat the oven to **400°F (205°C)**. Lightly grease a **deep 9×13-inch** baking dish. Set it on a rimmed baking sheet so any bubbling gravy stays out of the oven.

**Pan capacity matters:** The casserole is full-size. A deeper 3½–4-quart dish is safest. Keep roughly an inch of space between the hot filling and the rim before adding dough. Divide between two dishes if necessary.

### 2. Prepare the Vegetables and Chicken

Dice **3 carrots, 3 celery ribs, and 1 medium onion** to a similar small size so they cook evenly. Mince **3 cloves garlic** and pull **3 cups of cooked meat** from the rotisserie chicken. Keep frozen peas frozen until needed.

### 3. Soften and Brown the Vegetables

Place a large deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium heat. Melt **4 tbsp butter**; add carrot, celery, and onion. Cook **8–10 minutes**, stirring occasionally, until tender with a little browning. Add garlic, thyme, sage, and optional rosemary and cook **about 1 minute**.

### 4. Build the Creamy Gravy

Sprinkle **⅓ cup flour** evenly over the vegetables and stir **1 minute**. Pour in **2½ cups broth gradually while whisking**, scraping the bottom of the pan, then stir in **½ cup half-and-half**.

Simmer **3–5 minutes**, stirring, until thick enough to coat a spoon. Stir in smoked paprika, black pepper, prepared mustard, and optional pinches of turmeric and nutmeg. Add salt only **after tasting**.

**Texture target:** A thick, spoonable gravy, not watery soup. If it becomes gluey, loosen with a tablespoon or two of the reserved broth at a time.

### 5. Finish and Transfer the Filling

Fold in **3 cups cooked shredded chicken** and **1 cup frozen peas**. Simmer gently **2–3 minutes** until everything is hot. Spoon into the casserole, distributing chicken and vegetables across the pan.

**Do not fill to the rim.** If the filling is too deep for your dish, move a portion to a second oven-safe dish, cover with extra biscuit dough, and bake alongside.

### 6. Mix the Cheddar Biscuit Dough

In a large bowl, whisk **2 cups flour, 2¼ tsp sugar, 1 tbsp baking powder, ¾ tsp fine salt, 1½ tsp garlic powder, 1½ tsp dried oregano, and 1½ tsp dried parsley**. Omit cayenne for a mild family dinner.

Grate the **very cold ½ cup butter** directly into the flour, toss the shreds to coat, then gently fold in **1 cup cold buttermilk**. Fold in **1½ cups cheddar**. Stop mixing as soon as the dough holds together; a slightly rough, shaggy dough is correct.

**No grater?** Dice the butter into tiny cold cubes and pinch or fork them into the flour until pea-sized pieces remain.

### 7. Top and Bake

Scoop **10–12 portions** of dough onto the **hot filling**. Leave a little visible gravy between mounds for steam to escape.

Bake on the center rack at **400°F for about 30–35 minutes**. Begin checking at **25 minutes**. Done means:

- Biscuit tops are deep golden, not pale.
- Filling is bubbling around the edges.
- The thickest biscuit center is no longer wet or floury when gently opened or probed.

If the biscuit tops brown before the centers cook, cover loosely with foil and bake another 5 minutes at a time. If available, use a thermometer to verify the reheated filling reaches **165°F (74°C)**. Avoid touching a biscuit or pan with the probe.

### 8. Brush and Rest

Melt **¼ cup butter** with **1 tsp minced garlic**, warming about **30–60 seconds** until the garlic smells fragrant but is not browned. Stir in parsley and Parmesan. Brush or spoon onto the hot biscuit tops.

Rest **10 minutes** so the gravy settles and nobody burns their mouth on the bubbling filling. Serve by scooping a biscuit and a generous portion of creamy filling together.

## Shortcut Loadout: Boxed Cheddar Bay Biscuit Mix

For a lower-effort first cook, replace **all scratch-biscuit ingredients** with:

- **1 box Red Lobster Cheddar Bay Biscuit Mix (11.36 oz / 322 g)**, including seasoning packet
- **¾ cup cold water**
- **½ cup shredded sharp cheddar**
- **¼ cup melted butter** for the included garlic-herb seasoning packet

Prepare the mix according to the **box's dough instructions**, then drop approximately **8–10 biscuits** over the already-hot pot pie filling. The box specifies about **14–16 minutes at 425°F on a plain baking sheet**, but biscuits sitting on moist casserole filling at **400°F** can require **25–35 minutes or longer**. The biscuit center, not the package's plain-biscuit timer, determines doneness. Use the seasoning-packet butter topping and omit the recipe's separate scratch garlic-Parmesan finish unless desired.

**Choice:** The scratch dough more closely recreates Moribyan's herb-and-cheddar biscuits; the box is an easier pantry-and-equipment test. One box yields a lighter biscuit topping than the scratch version. If you want more biscuit per serving, bake extra boxed biscuits on a separate sheet following their package directions instead of burying the filling under an extra-thick dough layer.

## Substitutions and Family Options

- **No buttermilk:** Put **1 tbsp** white vinegar or lemon juice in a liquid measuring cup, fill with milk to **1 cup total**, stir, and let stand 5–10 minutes. This is a workable quick substitute; commercial buttermilk gives a somewhat better biscuit texture.
- **No half-and-half:** Use whole milk. For extra richness, use a splash of cream.
- **No fresh herbs:** The main formula already uses dried thyme, sage, and parsley. Skip rosemary if unavailable.
- **No rotisserie chicken:** Cook and shred boneless chicken first, ensuring chicken reaches 165°F (74°C). Do not stir raw chicken into the filling and assume the biscuit bake will safely cook it.
- **More vegetables:** Add ½–1 cup frozen corn, but reduce some other filling or use a larger dish to avoid overflow.
- **Kid-safe base:** Omit cayenne and red pepper flakes. Offer hot sauce or crushed red pepper at the table.
- **More pronounced cheddar:** Add a handful of cheddar to biscuit tops during the last 5 minutes if desired.
- **Equipment adaptation:** A large Dutch oven can go straight into the oven only if its handles and lid are rated for the temperature; otherwise transfer to the casserole.

## Doneness and Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Correction |
|---|---|
| Biscuits brown before their interiors are done | Loosely tent with foil; bake another 5–10 minutes. |
| Gravy is too thin before assembly | Simmer longer uncovered, stirring; remember it will thicken somewhat on resting. |
| Gravy is too stiff before assembly | Whisk in reserved broth 1–2 tbsp at a time. |
| Filling rises near pan rim | Stop adding filling and use a second dish. Keep a rimmed sheet pan underneath. |
| Biscuits dense / hard | Start with cold butter; stop mixing once dough holds together. |
| Too salty | Choose reduced-sodium broth and season only after adding rotisserie chicken. |

## Storage + Reheating

- **Within 2 hours of cooking**, divide leftovers into shallow, covered containers and refrigerate at 40°F (4°C) or colder.
- **Refrigerated leftovers:** Use within **3–4 days**; biscuit texture is best earlier.
- **Oven reheat:** At **350°F**, cover loosely and warm until the center of the filling reaches **165°F**; remove cover at the end to re-crisp biscuits. Timing varies with portion size and starting temperature.
- **Microwave reheat:** Cover loosely and warm in increments; verify 165°F in the filling. The biscuit will soften.
- **Freeze ahead:** Freeze the **cooled filling alone** for best quality, up to about **2 months**. Thaw safely in the refrigerator, heat through, then make fresh biscuit dough and bake.
- **Make ahead:** Cook the filling up to **2 days** ahead; cool promptly, refrigerate, reheat until thoroughly hot, and then add fresh biscuit dough just before baking.
