> **CousinStuff original adaptation.** Inspired by the Settlers of Foundation in Fallout 76. The fictional kitchen story below is not official game lore.

**Serves:** 8 | **Prep:** 15–20 minutes | **Cook:** 35–45 minutes | **Total:** ~60 minutes | **Equipment:** 6–8 qt pot or Dutch oven; small skillet; strainer

## From the Foundation Kitchen

At Foundation, there's always work to be done. Walls need reinforcing, crops need tending, and somebody has to make sure the generators keep running. By the time the sun disappears behind the mountains, a warm meal can be worth more than a pocketful of caps.

That's where Everything Soup comes in.

In our imagined Foundation kitchen, the cooks use whatever the gardens and supply runs brought in: carrots, potatoes, green beans, corn, poultry, and enough broth to bring everyone together. Nothing fancy, nothing wasted. Just a big pot shared among neighbors.

Our family has loved Everything Soup for years. This CousinStuff version preserves the original's colorful vegetables and signature rosemary-garlic butter, with a fuller broth, more chicken, and a coordinated cooking flow.

**Pull up a chair, Settler. There's plenty to go around.**

## Ingredients

### Aromatic base
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 1 small leek, white and light green parts only, thinly sliced and thoroughly rinsed
- 3–4 medium rainbow carrots, sliced into 1/4-inch coins
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 1/2 teaspoons chopped fresh thyme (or 3/4 teaspoon dried)
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 2 bay leaves
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt to start, plus more to taste

### Soup
- 7 cups low-sodium broth: preferably 5 cups chicken and 2 cups turkey; all chicken works
- 2 medium Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled if preferred, diced into 3/4-inch pieces
- 8 ounces fresh green beans, trimmed and cut into 1-inch pieces
- 1 cup frozen sweet corn
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 3 cups cooked shredded rotisserie chicken, both breast and thigh if available
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

### Rosemary-garlic finishing butter
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 2 teaspoons finely chopped fresh rosemary
- 1 garlic clove, lightly crushed
- Small pinch of salt

### At the table
- 1–2 teaspoons fresh lemon juice, to taste
- 2 tablespoons chopped fresh parsley
- Crusty bread or dinner rolls, optional

## Kitchen flow

1. **00:00–00:15 | Prep.** Dice the onion, carrots, pepper, potatoes, and green beans. Split and rinse the leek well. Mince garlic, chop herbs, shred cooked chicken, and measure broth. Keep ingredients grouped by when they'll enter the pot.
2. **00:15–00:25 | Sweat the vegetables.** Melt 2 tablespoons butter in a 6–8-quart pot over medium heat. Add onion, leek, carrots, bell pepper, and 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt. Cook 7–8 minutes, stirring, until starting to soften. Add minced garlic, thyme, paprika, and bay leaves; cook 1 minute until fragrant.
3. **00:25–00:38 | Simmer potatoes.** Add broth and potatoes. Bring just to a boil, then reduce to a gentle, partially covered simmer for 10–12 minutes, until potatoes are beginning to soften.
4. **00:35–00:43 | Infuse the finishing butter in parallel.** In a small skillet, warm 2 tablespoons butter over low heat with the rosemary, crushed garlic, and pinch of salt for 1–2 minutes, without browning. Turn off heat and steep 5 minutes, then strain. Reserve for serving.
5. **00:38–00:45 | Green beans.** Add green beans to the soup and simmer 4–5 minutes. Check potato tenderness; adjust timing if your cuts are larger.
6. **00:45–00:52 | Heat through.** Add chicken, frozen corn, and peas. Simmer 5–7 minutes until the vegetables are tender and the cooked chicken is heated all the way through (165°F when reheating previously cooked chicken).
7. **00:52–00:55 | Brighten and season.** Remove bay leaves. Stir in parsley and 1 teaspoon lemon juice. Taste and adjust salt, black pepper, and additional lemon as needed.
8. **Serve.** Ladle into bowls, add about 1/2–1 teaspoon strained rosemary-garlic butter per bowl, and serve with bread. For a gentler children's portion, serve without the finishing butter if preferred.

**Doneness checkpoint:** Potatoes should be fully tender without crumbling; green beans tender with some bite; poultry steaming hot. Simmer times are guides, not substitutes for checking the vegetables.

## Supply-run substitutions

- **No turkey broth:** Use all low-sodium chicken broth.
- **No rainbow carrots:** Use ordinary orange carrots. Color is part of the story, not a requirement.
- **No leek:** Add another small onion; flavor will be a little less delicate.
- **No fresh green beans:** Use frozen beans and shorten their simmer to avoid mushiness.
- **No fresh rosemary:** Use about 2/3 teaspoon dried rosemary in the finishing butter and strain carefully.
- **Stretch the pot:** Add 1–2 cups extra broth and another cup of vegetables; season again at the end.
- **More adult punch:** Add red pepper flakes, hot sauce, or more lemon only to individual bowls; keep the central pot family-friendly.

## Storage and reheating

Cool promptly and refrigerate in shallow, covered containers within 2 hours. Refrigerate 3–4 days or freeze up to 3 months for best texture. Frozen potatoes may soften after thawing. Reheat leftovers to 165°F, adding broth if thickened. Store remaining finishing butter separately in the refrigerator and use within the same 3–4-day leftover window.

## Serve with

- Toasted crusty bread or warm dinner rolls
- Simple side salad
- Extra parsley and lemon wedges for adult bowls

## CousinStuff field notes

- **Family baseline:** Mild broth, generous chicken and colorful vegetables; keep heat and acidity adjustable by bowl.
- **Signature step:** Rosemary-garlic finishing butter is an intentional link to the original family recipe; don't skip it in the first test batch.
- **Next cook test:** Record actual yield, salt level with your broth brands, texture of potatoes and beans, and how much finishing butter the children prefer.
- **Photo direction:** A practical enamel pot or dented camp-style stockpot, heaped ceramic bowls, bright vegetables, simple bread, a few subtle Foundation-workshop props. Keep the food appetizing and the Fallout references understated.
