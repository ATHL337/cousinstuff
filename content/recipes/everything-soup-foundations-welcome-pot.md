## From the Foundation Kitchen

At Foundation, there is always work to be done. Walls need reinforcing, crops need tending, and somebody has to make sure the generators keep running. When the sun disappears behind the mountains, a warm bowl can be worth more than a pocketful of caps.

In our imagined Foundation kitchen, Everything Soup begins with whatever the gardens and supply runs bring in: carrots, potatoes, green beans, corn, poultry, and enough broth to share with anyone who arrives hungry. Nothing fancy. Nothing wasted.

Our children have loved Everything Soup for years. This CousinStuff version preserves the colorful vegetables and signature rosemary-garlic butter of the soup they remember, while making it a substantially more broth-forward family meal.

**Pull up a chair, Settler. There's plenty to go around.**

## Cooking profile

**Flavor:** Mild, herb-forward, savory and generously brothy; individual bowls can be spicier.  
**Yield:** Approximately 10 hearty servings, or as many as 12 smaller bowls. Actual yield is unverified until the family's first test batch.  
**Total time:** About 80 minutes, including 25 minutes of prep. A wide 16-quart pot on a standard electric stove may take longer to reach a simmer.

### Equipment

- PrincessCackles' turquoise 16-quart Cocinaware tamale steamer, **with the steamer insert removed**; a conventional 10–12-quart stockpot also works.
- Large, appropriately sized electric-stovetop burner, a small skillet, a fine-mesh strainer, a ladle, measuring cups, cutting board and knife.
- Heat-resistant oven mitts. The Cocinaware handles can become hot.

**Wide-pot note:** This is a steamer-style pot, not a heavy Dutch oven. Keep the vegetable sauté at medium to medium-low heat, stir frequently, and avoid scorching the bottom. If the pot's flat bottom overhangs the heating element substantially, sauté the vegetables in a separate skillet and transfer them before adding broth.

## Broth options

Start with **12 cups (3 quarts)** of low-sodium broth: 8 cups chicken and 4 cups turkey, or all chicken. This is the new family baseline, replacing the older 7-cup version.

After adding all the chicken and vegetables, judge the consistency in the ladle. Warm and add 2 cups of extra low-sodium broth if it looks more like stew than soup. For a very broth-forward family table, add the remaining 2 cups.

| Version | Total broth | Recommended use |
|---|---:|---|
| Standard Foundation batch | 12 cups | Starting point for the first cook |
| Extra-brothy | 14 cups | More liquid for generous bowls and crusty bread |
| Maximum planned | 16 cups | For a decidedly broth-first supper |

The 16-quart stockpot has enough capacity for any of these planned amounts. **Taste before adding salt**, since broth brands vary widely in sodium. Do not add the optional reserve automatically.

## Supply-run substitutions

- **No turkey broth:** Replace with low-sodium chicken broth, cup for cup.
- **No rainbow carrots:** Ordinary carrots work perfectly.
- **No leek:** Substitute one additional small yellow onion.
- **No fresh green beans:** Use frozen and begin checking a few minutes sooner.
- **No fresh thyme:** Use 1 tsp dried.
- **No fresh rosemary:** Use about 2/3 tsp dried rosemary in the finishing butter and strain carefully.
- **No rotisserie chicken:** Use four cups of fully cooked, shredded chicken from another meal.
- **More heat:** Add hot sauce or chili flakes to individual adult servings, not the family pot.

## Doneness and troubleshooting

- **Potatoes:** Fully tender without falling apart. If still firm, simmer 3–5 minutes more.
- **Green beans:** Tender with a little bite. Frozen beans may require less time.
- **Cooked chicken:** Heated to 165°F before serving.
- **Soup too thick:** Add warm, low-sodium broth 1–2 cups at a time.
- **Broth seems flat:** Add a little lemon, fresh pepper or thyme before increasing salt.
- **Bottom is catching:** Reduce the burner, stir, and add a splash of broth. A large steamer-style pot can heat unevenly.
- **Rosemary butter too strong for the children:** Serve it separately; the soup remains a complete meal without the drizzle.

## Storage and reheating

Do not refrigerate the whole hot 16-quart pot. Divide the soup into shallow, covered containers promptly; refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking, at 40°F or below. An ice bath can help cool portions safely.

Refrigerate leftovers for **3–4 days**, or freeze individual portions for up to **3 months for best quality**. Potatoes can soften after freezing. Reheat leftovers to **165°F**, adding a little broth as needed. Keep any remaining rosemary butter separate in the refrigerator and use it within the same 3–4-day window.

## At the table

Serve with warm rolls, toasted sourdough or a simple green salad. Offer extra parsley, lemon and seasoning alongside the butter so each person can adjust their bowl.
