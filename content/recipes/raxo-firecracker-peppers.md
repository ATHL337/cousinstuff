## Overview

Raxo is a Galician pork dish built around a straightforward idea: small
pieces of pork, plenty of garlic, high heat, and the juices left behind
in the pan. Pair it with potatoes and blistered peppers and it becomes
exactly the kind of hearty, practical meal that feels at home around a
Wasteland camp table.

This CousinStuff version keeps the Galician foundation intact while
turning it into a complete eight-person family supper. The potatoes move
to the Ninja Foodi XL so the stovetop stays available for properly
searing the pork, while the peppers cook last and hit the table
blistered and hot.

## Pre-War Field Notes

This ration started far outside Appalachia.

During a 2026 family trip through Galicia, raxo and pimientos became one
of those deceptively simple combinations worth bringing home: garlicky
pork, potatoes, blistered peppers, good bread, and very little
unnecessary complication.

Back in the Texas Wasteland, the memory becomes **Raxo & Firecracker
Peppers**. The pork stays recognizably Galician, the potatoes become an
air-fryer workhorse, and shishitos provide a practical stateside
stand-in for pimientos de Padrón.

The result is not meant to turn Galician food into a Fallout gimmick. It
is a family travel memory preserved as a practical Wasteland supper.

-   3 1/2 lb boneless pork shoulder, trimmed and cut into 3/4--1-inch
    pieces
-   10 cloves garlic, finely minced or grated
-   2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
-   2 tbsp dry white wine
-   1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
-   1 tsp sweet Spanish paprika
-   1/2 tsp smoked Spanish paprika
-   1/2 tsp dried oregano
-   1/2 tsp black pepper

### Crispy Patatas

-   3 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, cut into roughly 3/4-inch chunks
-   2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
-   1 tsp kosher salt
-   1/2 tsp sweet Spanish paprika

### Firecracker Peppers

-   1 1/2 lb shishito peppers
-   1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
-   Coarse or flaky salt, for finishing
-   2 lemons, cut into wedges

### For the Table

-   1 large crusty loaf, sliced

## Instructions

### 1. Prep and Stage

Trim excess exterior fat from the pork shoulder and cut the meat into
3/4--1-inch pieces.

Cut the potatoes into roughly 3/4-inch chunks. Keep the pieces
reasonably uniform so they finish together.

Mince or grate the garlic.

Keep the shishito peppers whole.

Before cooking, divide the pork into **3--4 batches**. Do not wait until
the skillet is hot to make this decision.

Set a clean receiving platter or large bowl beside the stove for
finished pork.

### 2. Start the Crispy Patatas

Toss the potatoes with:

-   2 tbsp olive oil
-   1 tsp kosher salt
-   1/2 tsp sweet Spanish paprika

Divide the potatoes between the Ninja Foodi XL cooking zones. Avoid
packing them into a deep, tight layer.

Air fry at **400°F for 22--28 minutes**.

Toss or shake the potatoes about halfway through cooking.

The potatoes are done when tender inside with browned, crisp edges.
Their exact finish time can flex a few minutes to synchronize with the
stovetop.

### 3. Season the Raxo

While the potatoes begin cooking, toss the pork with:

-   Minced garlic
-   2 tbsp olive oil
-   1 tsp kosher salt
-   1 tsp sweet Spanish paprika
-   1/2 tsp smoked Spanish paprika
-   1/2 tsp dried oregano
-   1/2 tsp black pepper

Keep the pork divided into its cooking batches.

### 4. Sear the Raxo

Place a large heavy skillet over medium-high to high heat.

Once thoroughly hot, add the first pork batch in a **single uncrowded
layer**.

Let the meat develop color before moving it excessively.

Cook approximately **4--6 minutes per batch**, turning as needed, until
deeply browned and cooked through.

Transfer the finished pork to the receiving platter.

Repeat with the remaining batches.

If the skillet begins accumulating burned garlic rather than dark brown
fond, reduce the heat slightly.

### 5. Finish the Raxo

After the final batch, return all the pork and accumulated juices to the
skillet.

Add the **2 tbsp dry white wine**.

Scrape the browned bits from the bottom of the pan and toss everything
together for another **1--2 minutes**, until the liquid has mostly
reduced and coats the pork.

Taste and adjust the salt.

Transfer to the serving platter and cover loosely while the peppers
cook.

### 6. Blister the Firecracker Peppers

Heat the second skillet over high heat.

Add 1 tbsp olive oil.

Add the shishito peppers and cook for **5--7 minutes**, turning
occasionally.

The peppers should develop dark blisters and soften while retaining some
structure.

Transfer immediately to a serving dish and season generously with coarse
or flaky salt.

Most Firecracker Peppers are mild. Every once in a while, the Wasteland
reminds you who's in charge.

### 7. Final Crisp

Check the potatoes as the peppers finish.

If they need additional browning, give them another **2--4 minutes at
400°F**.

Taste one and adjust salt if necessary.

### 8. Serve

Bring the raxo, patatas, and Firecracker Peppers to the table
family-style.

Serve with:

-   Crusty bread
-   Lemon wedges

Let everyone build their own plate and use the bread to catch the
garlic-pork juices.

## Vault Notes

### Family Portion Strategy

This recipe deliberately makes one dinner rather than separate "adult,"
"diet," and "kid" meals.

A practical starting point is:

-   **Smaller adult portion:** 2 1/2--4 oz cooked pork, peppers, and a
    modest serving of potatoes
-   **Standard adult/older-kid portion:** 4--6 oz cooked pork with
    potatoes and peppers
-   **Younger-kid portion:** 2--3 oz pork with potatoes and bread
    according to appetite

Serve everything family-style and let appetite determine the final
plate.

### Pork Shoulder vs. Pork Loin

**Boneless pork shoulder is the default.** It has enough intramuscular
fat to stay juicy during high-heat batch cooking and gives the raxo a
richer pork flavor.

For a leaner version, use boneless pork loin, but watch it carefully.
Loin is less forgiving and should not be cooked beyond what is necessary
for food safety.

### Firecracker Pepper Note

Shishitos are used because they are widely available and behave
similarly at the skillet: quick cooking, blistered skin, and usually
mild heat with an occasional hotter pepper.

If genuine pimientos de Padrón are available, use them.

### Cut-Fit Switches

Pick based on the table and the day:

-   **Need leaner:** substitute trimmed boneless pork loin for shoulder.
-   **Need fewer starches:** take a smaller patata portion and load up
    on peppers.
-   **Need kid-friendly:** reserve part of the pork before adding the
    full garlic-and-paprika seasoning if necessary.
-   **Need more food:** add another pound of potatoes and a second loaf
    rather than crowding more pork into the skillet.
-   **Need less active cooking:** prep and season the pork and cut the
    potatoes earlier in the day.
-   **Need authentic peppers:** replace shishitos with pimientos de
    Padrón when available.

### Don't Crowd the Raxo

This is the most important cooking rule in the recipe.

Three and a half pounds of pork dumped into one skillet will release
enough moisture to steam itself. Smaller batches allow moisture to
escape and give the meat the browned exterior that makes the dish work.

### Microwave Contingency

The microwave is not part of the normal cooking plan.

If the potatoes are running significantly behind, microwave them in a
covered microwave-safe dish for **2--4 minutes**, then return them to
the Foodi to finish crisping.

Do not microwave the finished raxo unless reheating leftovers.

## Storage + Reheating

-   **Fridge:** store pork, potatoes, and peppers separately in airtight
    containers for up to 3 days.
-   **Raxo:** reheat briefly in a hot skillet with a splash of water,
    stock, or white wine.
-   **Patatas:** reheat in the Ninja Foodi XL at 375°F until hot and
    crisp.
-   **Peppers:** reheat briefly in a skillet or air fryer; expect them
    to soften after refrigeration.
-   **Microwave:** acceptable for the pork when convenience matters, but
    use short intervals to avoid drying it out.
-   **Freezer:** cooked raxo can be frozen, but potatoes and blistered
    peppers are best made fresh.
